####### Objective #############

The per-frame recipe

Run Orbis with its context (encoded past frames) at time step k.
The real next frame arrives. Encode it with the Orbis tokenizer into continuous latent tokens z*.
Do exactly what training does, without weight updates: pick a random noise level t and random Gaussian noise x₀, mix them: x_t = t·z* + (1−t)·x₀.
Feed x_t, t, and the context into the world model to get a predicted velocity.
The correct velocity is known in closed form: (z* − x₀). Compute the squared difference between predicted and correct velocity.
Repeat steps 3–5 for 4–8 different (t, x₀) pairs and average. That's the raw surprise score for frame k.

No generation, no denoising loop — just a few extra forward passes per frame.

Keep two extra views of the score:

Per noise level t. Errors at low noise reflect fine detail (sensor noise, artifacts); errors at heavy noise reflect coarse scene layout — that's where semantic surprises show up.
To make scores comparable across frames, use the same fixed set of t values (e.g., a small stratified grid) for every frame rather than fully random ones.
Per latent token. Orbis's latents are spatial, so the error forms a map over the frame.
A surprising object lights up its own region; a global condition shift (night, rain) raises the whole map. Localized spike = event; uniform rise = hard regime.

if I randomly draw one normal and one anomaly clip, what fraction of the time does the anomaly clip's z-score come out higher?

True Positive Rate (TPR) = (anomaly clips scoring above the threshold) / (total anomaly clips) — how many real anomalies you correctly catch
False Positive Rate (FPR) = (normal clips scoring above the threshold) / (total normal clips) — how many normal clips you incorrectly flag

conclusions

stronger detection power for anomalies that disrupt coarse scene layout/trajectory (oncoming, obstacles)
weaker power for anomalies that are localized-object or subtle-behavioral in nature (pedestrians, stationary events)
